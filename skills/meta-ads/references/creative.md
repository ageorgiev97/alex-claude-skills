## Why creative is the main lever
- Since the Andromeda retrieval system (announced December 2024, live in most accounts by late 2025), Meta picks the ad most likely to interest each individual instead of grouping people by interest. The creative now does the targeting: who an ad speaks to decides who sees it. [v055, v058]
- Inside one ad set Meta also sequences ads per person (for example an explanatory video first, then a static or testimonial to close) and skips steps for ready buyers. It can only do that when the ads differ from each other. [v003, v043, v066]
- Order of importance of ad elements: offer, creative, headline, format, primary text, call-to-action (CTA) button, description. A strong offer can carry an average ad; no ad rescues a weak offer. See `offers-and-economics.md`. [v061, v107]
- Spend rule: put about 10% of ad budget into creative (production or creator fees). It pays for itself with a 10% lift in return on ad spend (ROAS), and replacing do-it-yourself creative usually lifts ROAS 30–50%. [v128, v133]

## How many ads an ad set should hold
| Period | Guidance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2020–2021 | 3–5 ads per ad set, never more than about 5 live | v351, v477 |
| 2024 | Meta's guidance: 6 or fewer; 1–3 ads at $10–20 a day | v135 |
| Late 2025 | Cap dropped after Andromeda; 20+ per ad set became normal | v055 |
| 2026 (current) | Aim for about 20 distinct ads per ad set; 30, 50 or more live at once is fine | v008, v033, v043 |

**Current rule:** run as many high-quality, clearly different ads as the business can produce. There is no upper limit to respect; the constraint is production, not the platform. The reason is that Meta matches different ads to different people, and a person who sees a different ad each time fatigues more slowly than one who sees the same ad 7–8 times in 2–3 days. [v008, v033]

Conditions:
- **Quality gate.** Twenty is a target, not a prerequisite. Four strong, different ads beat 20 where 4–6 ideas are padded with filler. [v044, v085]
- **Small budgets and low conversion volume.** Launch with about 4 very different creatives plus a few variations, then add a couple at a time. A first-time advertiser can start with 2–3 simple variations. With only a few conversions a week Meta cannot learn across 20 ads at once. [v015, v044, v055]
- **Distinctness.** Near-duplicates (same image with a new background colour or text overlay, same video with a new hook) may be treated as one ad, with only one entered in the auction. Visually different ads are treated as separate. [v044]
- **Copy variants are not ads.** Put up to 5 primary texts, 5 headlines and 5 descriptions inside one ad; make separate ads only for different creative. [v043]
- **Concentrated spend is normal.** In an ad set of 5–20 ads, 1–3 usually take most of the budget. Keep ads Meta still spends on even if their own ROAS is lower, pause ads it has stopped spending on, and never switch off the top ad to force spend elsewhere. Mechanics are in `testing-and-scaling.md`. [v031, v048, v065]
- **Reference points.** A well-run account has 70–80 active ads; the most advanced have 300+. A competitor with 10–15 times your active ad count is a signal to raise production. [v021, v031]
- Many variants spread social proof thinly. Tick the option to combine likes and reactions for similar ads (All tools > "Advertising settings" > "Social information"; not in every account), and moderate ad comments every couple of days: delete negative ones, answer questions. [v052, v078, v084]

## Diversification: six axes
Diversity means ads that differ on the axes below. A new background colour, 20 statics in one template, or "all UGC" (user-generated content) is not diversity. It matters most for considered purchases and less for impulse products. Meta's figure: 5–10 assets against a single creative raised effectiveness by 44%. [v013, v055, v066]

| Axis | Vary across | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Format | static image, video, carousel, flexible, slideshow or animated static | All formats in the same ad set |
| Style | UGC, creator partnership, founder-led, demonstration, testimonial, proof-only, animation, meme, ASMR, podcast-style, myth-busting, FAQ, high production | Most accounts make one or two styles and miss the rest |
| Angle | the one main reason to buy (a desire or a pain point) | One angle per ad |
| Avatar | which sub-group the ad addresses and who appears in it | Segment in the message, keep targeting broad |
| Awareness stage | unaware to most aware | All stages to the same audience |
| Funnel role | introduce, give evidence, close | Cover all three in one ad set |

### Angles
1. Define the ideal customer narrowly: the most profitable 10–20% of customers, not everyone who could buy. Most businesses are too broad, not too niche. [v040, v042]
2. List the reasons that person would buy.
3. Give each ad one angle. Bundling angles hides which one works and makes the ad harder to follow. Most ads say too much, not too little. [v040]
4. Round one: one ad per angle (for example 5); keep the top 2. Later rounds, inside the winning angle: format, then style, then hooks, then copy. [v040]

Angle bank:
- Better result; saves time; saves money or better value; convenience and easy setup; less risk or anxiety; status; track record; exclusivity. [v039, v040]
- "We don't do what you hate about competitors": mine competitors' one-star reviews for it, and lead with what their five-star reviews praise if you offer it too. [v085]
- Easy, instant, simple, fast, hassle-free: the angle that wins hook tests most often. [v133]
- "New" beats "better" in sceptical markets that have tried everything. [v246]

The winning angle depends on the buyer. Owner-operators respond to results; a corporate buyer responds to a defensible, low-risk choice (credentials, partner status, press, testimonials from similar companies). For wealthy buyers lead with time, convenience, status and buy-once quality, not price. Decide which case the offer has to make: trust (proof), better than the old version, value for money, or time saved. [v040, v042, v107]

### Avatars
- Split one product into sub-groups and give each its own ad: a gym by age, fitness level, weight loss, muscle gain, sport, injury rehab; running shorts by cross-country, track, marathon, winter, hot weather; a service by industry or town. Ten ads that each speak to one slice beat one broad ad. [v003, v055, v121]
- Reframe the same offer per pain point (the freebie-seeker versus the person with decision fatigue) and use demographic call-outs such as "for men over 50". Airport fast-track sells as time saved, as not missing the flight, or as feeling VIP. [v021, v055]
- Put a person on screen who matches the avatar in age, gender and lifestyle; people watch and buy from people like them. [v056, v104]
- Local businesses: name the town in creative and copy and use visibly local reviews. See `local-and-high-ticket.md`. [v126]
- If a breakdown shows a group you want converting poorly, make creative that features and addresses that group; do not change targeting. Address one group exclusively only when it is about 93% of buyers; 83% is not enough. [v024, v118]

### Awareness stages
| Stage | The person | What the ad does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Unaware | Does not know they have the problem | Open on the problem ("Did you know X can be a real problem?"), then solution, why this product, why now. Model: "Were you injured at work? You could be owed up to..." |
| 2 Problem aware | Knows the problem, not the fix | "Do you have ABC problem? Did you know [solution] fixes it?" |
| 3 Solution aware | Knows the category | Show that this kind of product is the ideal form of the solution, done for them |
| 4 Product aware | Comparing you with alternatives | Benefits over competitors, proof, testimonials |
| 5 Most aware | Followers, past buyers | A product image plus a time-limited offer is enough |

- Run all stages to the same broad audience; do not segment audiences by stage. An ad pitched at a lower stage still converts more-aware people; the reverse fails. [v055, v124]
- Read decay as a depth problem: results die within days when the ads only convert stage 5; after a week or two when they stop at stage 4; they hold for weeks or months once stage 3 converts. Scaling while holding ROAS needs ads that convert stage 2. [v124]

### Funnel roles in one ad set
Include opener ads (founder story, why the company exists, problem-led), evidence ads (demonstration, how it works) and closer ads (testimonials, offer, strong CTA). When an ad is retired, replace it with one of the same role and stage. [v043, v064]

## Formats, and static versus video
| Format | Use for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Static image | Simple asks: impulse or low-price products, sales and discounts, lead magnets, free consultations; warm audiences; the bulk of volume | Cheap and fast to make |
| Video | Big asks: high-ticket offers, new inventions or concepts that need explaining, expertise or trust-based offers to cold audiences | A poor video loses to a good image |
| Carousel | Product ranges, items bought together, a series of results or testimonials | Link each card to its own page; write copy that fits the whole range; can raise order value; quick build from the best past images |
| Flexible | Image-heavy advertisers; Sales and App promotion objectives only (as of 2025) | See below |
| Slideshow, animated static | Product images with little text | Subtle-motion statics are a growing hybrid; Meta labels slideshows as video |

Catalogue ads: see `ecommerce.md`. Sources for the table: [v003, v080, v096].

**Static versus video, as of late 2026.** Statics now lead. In an October 2026 advertiser poll 54% named static their best format, 38% video, 8% other; earlier polls had video at 44%, then 53%, then 60%. Agency data across hundreds of accounts shows Meta allocating more budget to statics inside mixed-format ad sets. Causes: shorter attention, video drop-off (about 90% of viewers never reach the CTA) and statics standing out in video-heavy feeds. [v003]

What to do with that:
- Keep several formats in one ad set. Test both, let the winner take a larger share of the mix, and never drop the other. Do not go statics-only. [v003]
- Use statics to reach volume: 20 ads as 16 statics and 4 videos is easier to sustain than 10 and 10. [v003]
- Make statics more specific than video: one clear message for one buyer persona, readable instantly. [v003]
- Match format complexity to the size of the ask. Video converts better among those who watch, but far fewer watch. [v096, v347]
- Do not judge format by click-through rate (CTR): statics often get more clicks while video pre-sells better. Judge on ROAS or cost per result. [v118]

**What a good static contains:** (1) a high-contrast visual on a clean background, not over-designed; (2) one value proposition showing the transformation, not the specification; (3) a proven layout: side-by-side comparison, old way versus new way, product or outcome at its best, review screenshot, result screenshot, problem and solution; (4) an offer, the most important element. Avoid before-and-after images of bodies (policy; see `account-health-and-policy.md`). A static with good ROAS but low CTR (0.55% in one case) is not stopping the scroll: test brighter colours or clearer product focus. [v015, v024, v111]

**Flexible format** ("Flexible" under Format at ad level). Upload up to 10 creatives to one ad; Meta shows them singly, animated, as a slideshow or together as a carousel, and handles aspect ratios. Unlike dynamic creative it is an ordinary ad that sits beside other ads in the ad set. In tests Meta nearly always gives it more budget than regular image ads and it typically does better. [v082]
- Every creative must work alone and side by side. Use different images of one product or range, or separate benefits each strong enough alone. No near-identical variants and no cards that only make sense together.
- Write primary text and headline that fit any single creative and all of them together.
- Enhancements are all-on or all-off here; leave them on unless strict brand presentation is essential.
- It starts slowly: weeks 1–2 are often clearly worse than weeks 4–8. Do not kill it after a few days. It fatigues more slowly.

## Styles and proof
| Style | What it is | Fits, and cautions |
|---|---|---|
| UGC | An ordinary person or customer with no following, lo-fi, reads as a peer recommendation | Impulse ecommerce and local trades. A hard sell makes it feel paid. Wrong for premium or expert positioning [v050, v084] |
| Creator partnership | Someone with a following and trust in the niche | Often the best ad in the account. See below |
| Founder-led | Founder to camera: why the business exists, what the product does | Services and expertise. Needs eye contact, light and sound; cut introductions from the opening [v050, v058] |
| Demonstration | Product or service in use; old way versus new way; time-lapse | New or misunderstood products; visual services, where the ad is the proof of quality [v050, v058, v080] |
| Testimonial | One or several customers on video, or review statics | Any business. Local: show the reviewer's name and town [v126] |
| Proof-only | Quick-cut compilation of customer reactions or results, no pitch | Offers understood from context (physio, restaurant, kitchen fitter). The copy must explain the offer [v111] |
| Content-style | Podcast clip, myth-busting, FAQ, common mistakes, behind the scenes, day in the life | Feels like content, not an ad [v021, v059] |
| Meme, ASMR, funny-clip edit | High variance | Can damage premium brands; keep only if cost per result is much better [v041] |
| Animation, slideshow | Rare, so it stands out | Cheap to commission [v084] |
| High production | Polished brand film | Premium products. Do not rely on one expensive hero video with afterthought statics [v021] |
| Repurposed organic post | A top organic post or Reel with a 5–10 second CTA added | Already market-tested [v029, v042, v121] |

Style list: [v013, v021, v039].

- **Production value must fit the story.** Lo-fi works when a customer or creator recommends an impulse product or a local trade. It hurts when you present yourself as the expert or sell something premium or digital. [v041, v050]
- **Proof belongs in nearly every ad**, from about 25% to 80–90% of its length. Types: video or written testimonials, result screenshots, filmed results (harder to fake, so better in sceptical markets), finished-work clips, sequential transformations. Proof-only compilations more than doubled ROAS for a clinic chain with the same offer and budget. [v111]

## Hooks
The hook is the first 3 seconds: opening scene plus opening line. Its job is to sell watching the rest, not the product. Target reaction: "that's me" or "that applies to me". [v104]
- 90–95% of viewers never get past it, so it decides whether the offer is seen. Moving hook rate from 8% to 33% puts the message in front of about 4 times as many people; changing only the hook can double ROAS. [v039, v063, v097]
- Hook rate = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions (video only). Above 10% is good, 5–10% acceptable, below 5% poor. Best ads in a lead-generation account reached 26–27%; a recognised creator reached about 35%. It varies by industry (B2B is lower), so compare against the account's own average. As of late 2026 hook rate and hold rate are native metrics. See `analysis-and-troubleshooting.md`. [v007, v114, v118]

| Verbal hook type | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Call-out by location, profession, life stage or spend level | "Texans, did you know..."; "For people living in West London"; "If you're tired of stocking your fridge with bottled water" | Test several levels of specificity. Name customer types rather than "all kinds of businesses" [v041, v046, v104] |
| Question the prospect answers yes to | "Do you struggle to get your Facebook ads to work?"; "Do you spend too much time cooking and cleaning?"; "Do you dream of changing careers?" | Never an optional question that invites a no ("Does anyone like this...?") [v046, v079, v104] |
| Conditional statement | "If you spend more than $100,000 per month on Facebook ads, we guarantee a 30% improvement in ROAS in 3 months"; "If you can't walk your dog in the park because of reactivity, we have the perfect programme for you" | Usually beats the question form; test both [v079, v104] |
| Outstanding result | "See how this client added $100,000 of value to their home with our solar panels"; "How this Texan mum lost 50 lb in 6 months without exercising" | Must be true. Also works as a headline [v079, v104] |
| Buyer call-out | "Best gift my boyfriend has ever got me" | Names who buys, with the product in shot [v050] |
| Pattern interrupt | "Wait, did no one tell you..." | Pair with movement [v046] |
| Positioning claim | "The only kit that actually replaces a salon" | Sets up savings against the alternative [v046] |
| Stakes | "If you're a business owner who thinks having a website is enough, here's why you're about to be left behind" | Problem plus consequence [v050] |
| Contrarian or insider | "Everything you know about X is wrong"; common mistakes; behind the scenes | [v059] |
| Deal | "You're not going to believe this deal" | Genuine big sales only; converts warm audiences, not cold [v050] |
| Story or curiosity | "I thought something was wrong with my order..." | Takes 10–12 seconds to reveal the product; only for known brands or creators [v046] |

**Visual hooks:** movement (walking, camera moves, handheld phone, calling the camera over); bright clothing or background; outdoor or striking locations; a setting that matches the prospect (a dental practice for dentists, a chaotic family kitchen for busy parents); the problem shown on screen; a local landmark; a prop (whiteboard, laptop, results screenshot); the product in use; a recognisable face, which gives the highest hook rates of all. The best hooks pair a spoken call-out with an attention-grabbing visual that is relevant to the product. [v050, v079, v104]

Rules:
- The hook must lead into what follows; a misleading hook shows as high hook rate with low play time. [v041, v118]
- No dead air: start speaking at once and trim the start and end of the clip. [v046, v050]
- Do not address sensitive personal attributes directly. Use third person ("Young men who are anxious often struggle with...") instead of "Are you suffering from anxiety?". Meta also rejects shocking openings. See `account-health-and-policy.md`. [v104]

**Hook multiplication.** Record a few full bodies and many hooks, then edit each hook onto each body: 2 bodies × 10 hooks = 20 ads for roughly the work of 3; 10 visual × 10 verbal hooks × 2 bodies = 200 combinations. A hook variant is a new opening line, scene, camera angle, location or outfit, and it must look clearly different. Hook-only variants can be suppressed as duplicates, so run them through the creative testing tool (`testing-and-scaling.md`). [v044, v063, v079]

**Hook and body recombination.** Sort video ads by hook rate next to cost per result. High hook rate with poor cost per result means a good hook on a weak body; low hook rate with strong cost per result means a weak hook on an excellent body. Splice the first onto the second. Needs a fair sample (the examples had 33 and 64 conversions) and a hook that fits the body. [v024, v038]

## Video structure, length and Reels specifications
Short direct-response template (about 20 seconds): [v020]

| Seconds | Section |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Hook |
| 3–8 | Problem |
| 8–15 | Solution |
| 15–18 | Proof |
| 18–20 | CTA |

Other proven orders:
- Longer script: hook, problem agitation, revelation with proof, solution, CTA. [v059]
- Local service (a 37-second ad that ran about 2.5 years): location and problem call-out, agitate, the practitioner with credentials, reviews, offer, scarcity, specific CTA ("click the Get offer button below"). [v041]
- Explainer: desired outcome shown first, how it works, who it is for, why it beats the alternatives. [v058]
- Expert to camera: problem question, "not your fault" reframe, agitation, the offer in three steps, CTA, value restated. [v050]
- Third-party UGC, about five sentences: audience and problem, name the product, social proof, three things you get, credibility marker, CTA, closing benefit. [v056]
- Proof-first: about 40 seconds of customer results, then about 10 seconds of offer. [v111]

Craft rules:
- Open with a demonstration of the outcome; move introductions and backstory later. [v058]
- For cold audiences say what the product is quickly. Opening on an objection or a clever story suits warm audiences. [v046]
- Give benefits with numbers, several use cases, and answers to the main objections (time, price, difficulty, cancellation). [v046, v050]
- Use numeric or third-party proof ("200,000+ people switched", a named publication's rating). [v046]
- Hold attention with cuts that each show the product, B-roll (supporting footage), and a jarring visual or edit at about 8 seconds, when attention drifts. [v050]
- End with an explicit CTA in audio or on-screen text plus an offer with urgency (deadline) or scarcity (limited stock or slots). The more specific the CTA in the creative, the better the results; a missing CTA is the most common fault. Service businesses can use capacity scarcity truthfully. [v041, v046, v050]
- For an evergreen UGC ad, leave out dated discounts so a winner can run all year. Decide this deliberately. [v050]
- Use a native-level speaker for the market, one voice throughout (no switching to a generic AI voice), and "affordable" rather than "cheap". Match tone to brand: clickbait wins short term and costs a premium brand. [v046, v050]

Length: as long as needed and no longer. Small asks take short videos; a 4 min 47 s service walk-through belonged in 30–60 seconds. Proof compilations ran from 15–20 seconds to 2 minutes (best about 90 seconds); do not judge those on average watch time. Big asks to cold audiences justify longer video with proof. [v041, v096, v111]

**Reels specifications.** Ads built for Reels get 13% better ROAS and 16% lower cost per acquisition (CPA) on average (Meta data). [v066, v074]
- Vertical 9:16, full screen. If only one ratio can be made, make this one. Ideally also upload a purpose-made square version with "Replace" in the crop step instead of letting Meta crop; most impressions are square or vertical, few horizontal. [v029, v036, v066]
- Build for sound on, and add audio to silent or slideshow creative. Keep captions and bold text overlays for muted viewers. [v015, v066]
- Keep text, offer details and captions inside the Reels safe zone, which is less than half the frame and smaller than the Stories safe zone. Keep captions mid-frame, away from the top and bottom. The centre is also what survives a crop to feed. Brief creators on this. [v036, v066, v074]

## Ad copy
**Slots.** Up to 5 primary texts, 5 headlines and 5 descriptions per ad; fill several. Leave "Text improvements" and "Optimize text per person" on so Meta picks the combination per person and placement; turn them off only in regulated industries where rearranged wording could breach rules. Every text must then make sense in any position. [v036, v043, v052]

**Primary text rules:** [v015, v102, v365]
- Open with a hook of about 6–10 words: a call-out, a bold claim or a problem.
- Put the offer in the first 3 lines, above "See more", and say exactly what is offered.
- Keep it as long as needed and no longer: 1–3 lines for a cheap product, more for a complex or new one. Break benefits onto separate lines.
- Write to one person, conversationally, with one CTA.
- Be specific: no internal product names, no vague lines such as "Unlock your business potential". State a price when it is a selling point; stating the job size ("40K+ install") repels bargain hunters.
- In sceptical categories add short named customer quotes. [v102]
- Match the audience named in the copy to the one named in the creative. No hashtags, no typos. [v041, v050]
- Repeat incentive and deadline in primary text, creative and headline, and on the landing page. [v244]
- When the video is pure proof, the copy must explain the offer. [v111]
- Put stronger result claims inside the creative rather than the text, where automated review flags them more readily; they must be true. See `account-health-and-policy.md`. [v112]

| Formula | Order |
|---|---|
| Short direct | Question naming the audience or problem, agitate, feature, benefit, scarcity, CTA [v049] |
| Service | Question naming the outcome without the work, what we do, proof, CTA with link [v102] |
| Sceptical market, long form | Credential line, 3 short named customer quotes, list of what you get [v102] |
| Price-anchored | Benefit with price ("feedback for under $60 per session"), what is included, outcome, scarcity [v029] |
| Local service | Call-out, two simple benefits, low-risk offer: "Moving house? Redecorating? We'll design a beautiful interior and manage the project for you", then a free, no-obligation consultation [v519] |
| Range or carousel | One line that fits every card: "Men's real leather shoes made in the UK, from only £45" [v365] |

**Headline.** Always write one. For cold audiences be descriptive and lead with the offer: "20% off luxury leather gloves", "Book your free interior design consultation", "FREE personal training session", "50% off personal tax returns this month", not "Free consultation" or "Award-winning accountancy practice". Key information first. Use elevating adjectives where true (premium, bespoke, handmade, clinically proven). No clever or insider headlines; people read them distracted. Vague or personal headlines such as "Want me to mentor you?" work only on warm audiences who know the brand. [v102, v377]

**Description:** a scarcity or urgency line ("Limited spots available"); it is not shown on every placement. **CTA button:** choose the one that names the next step ("Shop now", "Sign up", "Schedule a call"; "Learn more" when the page carries a sales video). Low impact; test it last. [v029, v049, v107]

**AI text suggestions.** Write one primary text and one headline yourself, then review Meta's variations one by one; typically 2 or 3 of 5 are usable. AI primary text is often fine; AI headlines are usually worse. Reject generic wording, the wrong audience temperature, a mix-up between your offers, internal names and factual errors. [v097, v102]

## Creator and partnership ads
A partnership ad runs from your ad account, with your budget and targeting, and is shown from the creator's handle as well as or instead of the brand's. Meta's data: +13% CTR and +71% brand lift against non-partnership ads. The best ad in an account is often a creator ad; changing only the person on screen has moved ROAS from 2.5 to 6.5. [v019, v023, v074]

Why it works: followers stop for a face they know (higher hook rate), the recommendation carries weight after the click, and the creator already knows how to make content. UGC is not the same thing: a UGC actor has no following and lends no trust. [v013, v033]

Workflow:
1. **Find.** All tools > "Engage audience" > "Creator Marketplace" (free, 1.5M+ creators as of 2026, Facebook and Instagram). Needs a professional Instagram account connected to a Facebook Page; some accounts must request access. Start with "Recommendations", creators already active in partnership ads, and creators who follow or tagged you. Messages sent there land in a separate brand inbox, so they get read. [v013, v023, v061]
2. **Select on fit, not size.** The creator's audience must be your buyer, and the creator must be known for the topic. Small niche creators beat celebrities; 5,000 relevant followers has value. Scale the name with the budget. [v019, v023, v061]
3. **Vet.** Compare followers with "accounts engaged" in the last 30 days (about 3% engaged is a red flag); check audience age, country and gender, hook rate (49% is very good) and interaction rate. Check for past controversies and past comments about your product. [v023, v061]
4. **Offer.** State the fee, the exact deliverable and what must be said, and send free product. Typical figures: about $100 from very small creators; $800 plus product for two review videos (a 175,000-follower creator, 263% ROAS gain); $1,000 for two Reels (illustrative). The fee replaces videographer cost. Paying 10% of ad budget to creators is a good trade when it makes the campaign 40% more effective, which happens repeatedly. Payment is settled directly; Meta takes no cut. [v033, v061, v088]
5. **Always secure permission to run the content as ads** ("Ad boost permission" in a marketplace brief). An organic post is seen for about 24 hours; an ad runs for months. Pay little extra for it (up to about $500 on a $1,000 deal, often nothing). [v023, v061]
6. **Start with 2–4 creators.** It is hit and miss; one flop means the wrong person, not a failed approach. [v023, v114]
7. **Run it as a partnership ad:** ad level, toggle "Partnership ad" under the ad name, then select the partner or enter the partnership ad code. Running the raw files from your own page also works but loses the creator's handle. [v046, v061, v081]
8. Ask for versions pitched at cold audiences. Discount-led creator ads convert warm audiences and do not scale. [v050]

## UGC and AI-made creative
- UGC suits impulse purchases because it reads as a peer recommendation. Keep it natural: heavy B-roll and a hard sell undermine it. Real UGC takes months of outreach, and 90%+ of what comes back is weaker than hoped or unusable. [v050, v056, v080]
- AI-looking images perform badly. AI for product backgrounds or on-model shots is fine when undetectable. [v003]
- Meta's AI image variations at upload: keep background and colour changes; reject any that swap or distort a person. Meta displayed an estimated 11% CTR lift and 8% conversion-rate lift for using them. [v036, v097]
- Do not use generated imagery where the claim depends on real results or the images show real work (service portfolios, results-based products). [v019, v024]
- AI actors: use them first to make variations of a proven video, then the same script with several actors, one per avatar. Never fake a testimonial: the actor must not claim to have used the product. Script it as a third party passing on true, verifiable facts. [v056]
- Ads Creative Studio (All tools > "Advertise"; new in late 2026): shows why creatives miss editable "minimum performance thresholds" (one account: image CTR 2.92%, video about a point higher), compares with industry top performers, and under "Scale top creatives" generates variants of winners: new video hooks (first 3–5 seconds), subtitles, AI voiceover, resized or animated images, new on-image text. Review every output. [v007]
- For AI ideation give full context: who you are, what the product includes, the step after the click, the audience and its knowledge level; ask for 10 ideas with several hooks each. Bare prompts return what competitors also get. [v059]
- AI raises everyone's floor, so the edge moves to trusted people and brand: creator content gains value as feeds fill with generated content. [v053, v074]

## Creative enhancement settings
Location: ad level > ad creative > "Set up creative" > enhancements. Meta pre-selects some and lists others "for review". The set differs by format and asset and changes often (names as of 2026). Decide per creative and check every preview. Expect little: Meta's own estimate for the standard bundle was about 3% lower cost per result. Enhancements matter more for images than video. [v024, v036]

**Warning: enhancements switch themselves back on.** Duplicating an ad, editing it (even only the primary text or headline) or just previewing it can re-enable enhancements you turned off. After touching an ad, reopen the enhancements and re-check. The risks are off-brand overlays, AI variations that alter faces, and altered claims or visuals in regulated industries. If you edit or duplicate an ad for the user, tell them the enhancements need re-checking. [v012]

| Enhancement | Setting | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Visual touch-ups" (auto crop or expand) | On | Covers more placements and newer ratios. Design for it: key content away from the edges. Off if a crop cuts text |
| "Flex media" | On | Lets Meta use your vertical asset where it fills more screen |
| "Text improvements", "Optimize text per person" | On | Per-person text combinations. Off in regulated industries |
| "Enhance CTA" | On, after reviewing the phrases | Non-standard CTAs can stand out; delete any you dislike |
| "Add animation" | On for visual images with little text; off for text-heavy images | Movement grabs attention, but text must stay still to be read |
| "Add overlays" | On for text-free product or service images; off if the image already has text | Overlays help clean images and clutter busy ones |
| "Music" | On for silent creative, with a track you chose; otherwise optional | Not a needle mover; the default track is often a poor fit |
| "Add video effects" | Off for graded footage; on for dull footage | Colour and sharpening only |
| "Enhance media text" (beta) | Off when on-image text carries meaning (testimonials, claims); testable on a short single-benefit overlay | AI rewrites the text in the image |
| AI image or background generation, image expansion | On for product shots after reviewing each; off where a real person or real work is shown | It can invent people or work that does not exist |
| "Translate text" | Off unless you serve those languages | |
| "Personalized destinations" | Off | Meta may send clicks to a different URL, which breaks planned funnels and muddies page analysis. Tolerable for stores sending traffic to product pages |
| Site links, promotions, catalogue items | Off unless there is a reason | Low impact |

Sources: [v012, v036, v049]. Flexible ads are the exception: all on. [v082]

## Fatigue and refresh
Fatigue is the "I've seen this before" filter: people scroll past an ad they recognise. It is mostly hook fatigue. [v079, v115]

| Signal | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Exposures to the same ad | After about 4, conversions can fall about 45% (Meta data) | v115 |
| Average frequency | Near 4 or above: act now. Damage starts lower, because the average hides best prospects at 6–7 | v115 |
| Cold-audience frequency | Above about 2.5 is a concern | v118 |
| Warm-audience frequency | Up to about 6–8 for most businesses | v118 |
| Pattern | Results were good, then fall as frequency climbs | v007, v118 |

Check frequency at campaign level, then ad level; similar-looking ads share fatigue. Meta's AI business assistant and its "creative fatigue" recommendation flag the same pattern; confirm with frequency and run time before acting. High total frequency across varied creative is fine. If results fall although creative is being refreshed, suspect offer fatigue (`offers-and-economics.md`). [v007, v115, v121]

Refresh in this order, cheapest first:
1. **New look, same script and hook:** new background or location; new outfit or look; a different person who fits the market; reordered selling points after the hook; a filter or effect; a new editing or caption style or music; a different tone or speed (about 1.2× or 0.85×). [v116]
2. **New visual hook on the same verbal hook and body.** This can stretch a 6-week ad to 6 months or more. [v079]
3. **New hooks** on the winning body, including Ads Creative Studio variants. [v007, v063]
4. **New ads modelled on the winner:** same angle or style, new execution. [v065]
5. **Rest and reuse.** A fatigued ad can be reactivated about 6 months later. [v122]

New creative must look visually different or it fails the same filter. Audiences that renew themselves (new parents) fatigue slowly; fixed hobbyist or local audiences fatigue fast. [v114, v121]

## Production cadence and idea sources
| Situation | Cadence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Small budget | About one meaningful creative test a month | v014 |
| Baseline for any account | At least 4 new ads a month; more with more spend or offers | v084 |
| Local or small audience | An ad tires after about a month; a new local-event offer every 2–4 weeks supplies fresh creative | v126 |
| $100,000+ a month | Fixed weekly output, for example 12 new creatives a week across 3 proven types (testimonials, creator partnerships, simple statics); niche audiences can fatigue in days; extreme accounts add creative 4 times a week | v014, v121 |
| Top operators | 50+ new ads a week per business | v008 |

Set a schedule the business can sustain and keep it when winners appear; the usual failure is stopping production, then scrambling when winners fatigue. Systemise testimonial capture. Budget growth needs creative growth: one winner will not hold at 10 times the spend. [v044, v065, v084]

Idea sources: the account's own top ads over the last 30 days (formats, hooks, segments), then 5 variations of each winner; top organic posts; the Ads Library; competitors' reviews; adjacent industries; a swipe folder of ads that stopped you. [v020, v042, v085]

## Modelling competitors through the Ads Library
1. Open the Meta Ads Library. Set location to where the ads are shown, category "All ads", and search by advertiser page name. Set active status to "Active and inactive" to see retired ads too. Watch for impersonator pages. [v031, v101]
2. Sort by "Impressions: high to low". High impressions plus a long run time is the closest outside signal of a winner. A long run time alone no longer proves anything, because many live ads get no spend; an ad launched last week proves nothing either. [v031, v042]
3. Read: number of active ads, formats and styles, hooks, primary text, headlines, variations, start dates, platforms. Ads delivered in the EU also show age, gender and reach. [v031, v101]
4. Click the CTA and follow the funnel: page type, offer, price, upsells. [v031]
5. Model the format, structure, hook type and angle; write your own words and shoot your own footage. Copies land weaker (the audience has seen that hook) and may clash with your voice. [v059, v101]
6. Skip what you cannot reproduce and look at smaller competitors until you find something achievable. [v031]

## Briefs
**Creator brief** (specific on outcome, loose on delivery; tightly scripted reads perform worse): [v013, v023, v061]
- Deliverables: 2–3 full videos, each with about 5 hook variations (a different opening line and a different scene, location, angle or outfit). Many creators will record up to 10 for little extra. Reels format, 9:16, safe zones respected.
- Must-haves: the CTA, the key benefit or two, credibility markers from the offer.
- Left to the creator: script, style, visuals, delivery. Ask for one product demonstration and one "why I use this" video if useful.
- Terms: fee, free product, dates, permission to run as partnership ads and for how long.

**Ad brief** (fields to fill for any new ad): offer and landing page; avatar; awareness stage; one angle; format and style; 3–5 verbal hooks and visual hooks; structure with timings; proof to show; spoken, on-screen and button CTA; specifications (9:16, safe zone, sound on, captions); 5 primary texts and 5 headlines; policy cautions.

## Checklist for reviewing an ad
1. Is it clear within about 2 seconds what this is and who it is for? [v015, v133]
2. One message and one angle, not a feature list? [v040]
3. Hook: movement or a pattern interrupt plus a call-out, relevant to the product, no dead air, no question that invites a no? [v046, v050]
4. Benefits stated with numbers; objections pre-empted; use cases shown? [v046]
5. Proof present (numeric, third-party, testimonial, result)? [v111]
6. A specific offer, with urgency or scarcity that is true? [v050, v244]
7. An explicit CTA in the creative, matching the button and headline? [v046]
8. Production level, tone and overlay design fit the positioning (no cheap-looking banners or emoji on a premium offer)? [v041, v050]
9. Copy and creative address the same audience; headline descriptive; no hashtags or typos? [v041, v050]
10. Built for Reels: 9:16, sound, captions, safe zone? [v066]
11. Does the landing page repeat the ad's offer and look? See `offers-and-economics.md`. [v084]
12. Is it clearly different from the ads already live? [v044]
13. Policy: no direct personal attributes, no before-and-after bodies, no engagement bait. [v078, v104, v111]

## What creative is this account missing?
Place the account on the five-level scale, then name the gaps. The full audit is in `analysis-and-troubleshooting.md`. [v021]

| Level | Signs | What moves it up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ads repurposed from TV or billboards, corporate statics, no CTA, hashtags | Make ads for the platform with a CTA |
| 2 | Decent ads, broken after the click: generic multi-product page, product looks different from the ad | One matching landing page per ad or product |
| 3 | One style repeated, vague CTA, one expensive hero video and afterthought statics | Add styles and formats; specific CTAs |
| 4 | 70–80 active ads mixing UGC, high production, creator partnerships and review statics; one offer and page for everyone | Reframe the offer per avatar and pain point |
| 5 | 300+ active ads, very wide style range, the same offer reframed per pain point, demographic call-outs, landing pages matched to each ad | Maintain cadence |

Gap questions to answer from the account: How many active ads, and how many are distinct? What share is static, video, carousel? Which styles are absent? How many angles, avatars and awareness stages are covered? Do winning videos have hook variants? Is there a creator ad? Does every ad carry proof and a specific CTA? Then read the metrics:

| Pattern | Creative fix |
|---|---|
| Some conversions, low hook rate and low CTR | Creative is not grabbing attention: new hooks, new styles (the most common case) [v025] |
| High hook rate or CTR, weak ROAS | Good hook, weak body: move the hook onto a better body [v024, v038] |
| Low hook rate, strong cost per result | Excellent body: give it a proven hook [v038] |
| Good hook rate and play time, link CTR under 0.5% | Add proof or strengthen the offer [v118] |
| Good ROAS, low CTR on a static | Brighter, clearer visual [v024] |
| Results decay within days or a couple of weeks | Add ads for less-aware stages [v124] |

## Outdated advice to ignore
| Old rule | Current rule | Why it changed |
|---|---|---|
| No more than 5–6 ads per ad set | About 20 as the aim, 50+ acceptable, no upper limit | Andromeda matches ads to individuals and needs options [v043, v055] |
| Video is the best format; make video first | Statics lead as of late 2026; run mixed formats | Attention spans, video drop-off, statics stand out and are cheaper to make in volume [v003] |
| Test formats to pick one winner | Run statics, video and carousel together | Meta matches and sequences formats per person [v039] |
| A separate ad for each headline or primary text | 5 primary texts, 5 headlines, 5 descriptions inside one ad | Ad counts explode otherwise [v043] |
| Pause every ad with lower ROAS; split-test down to one best ad | Keep ads Meta still spends on | They open the funnel or win other slices of the audience [v005, v065] |
| Separate creative per funnel-stage campaign, or for cold and warm | All stages and roles in one ad set | Meta sequences per person [v043] |
| If you can make one ratio, make 1:1 | Make 9:16 | Vertical placements now matter more than feed [v066] |
| Build for sound off | Build for sound on, keep captions | More people watch with sound, especially in Reels [v066] |
| Model the oldest still-active ads in the Ads Library | Sort by impressions, then check run time | Many live ads get no spend [v031] |
| "Text improvements" off for advanced advertisers | On, except in regulated industries | Meta picks text per person and placement [v036] |
| Set enhancements once | Re-check after every duplicate, edit or preview | They re-enable themselves [v012] |
| No testimonials in primary text | Short named quotes help in sceptical categories (courses, communities, big decisions) | Quotes answer the doubt that AI or generic copy cannot [v102] |
| Pay an influencer to post on their own feed | Get content you run as partnership ads | A post lasts about a day; an ad runs for months [v023] |
| Run an Engagement campaign first to build social proof | Launch in the Sales or Leads campaign; pool reactions across similar ads | You pay for engagement on ads that may not convert [v078] |
| Dynamic creative for small visual variants | Creative testing tool for near-duplicates; flexible format for sets of different images | Near-duplicates get suppressed; dynamic creative limits the ad set to one ad [v044, v082] |
| Judge creative on CTR, hook rate or watch time | Decide on ROAS or cost per result; use the rest to explain why | They often do not correlate with results [v118] |
