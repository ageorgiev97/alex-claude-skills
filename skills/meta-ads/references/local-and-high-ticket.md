# Local, service and high-ticket businesses

How to advertise a business that serves a limited area, sells through calls, quotes or consultations, or has a customer worth about $1,000 or more. Read it when planning or auditing such an account, when deciding whether an awareness campaign made of many small ad sets is a mistake or a deliberate omnipresent content campaign, and when brand or organic content is part of the question.

## Which strategy fits which business

| Business | Main campaign | Add alongside |
|---|---|---|
| Local, modest price per visit, repeat custom (clinic, salon, gym, restaurant) | Leads or Sales campaign built on a strong, time-limited local offer, often a cheap paid first step | Nothing until the offer works. Omnipresent content only if lifetime value reaches about $1,000 |
| Local, high-ticket service (roofing, remodelling, interior design, legal) | Leads campaign to a quote, survey or consultation | Omnipresent content |
| National service or expertise business (agency, coach, consultant, course, professional service) | Leads campaign to a call, application, webinar or lead magnet | Omnipresent content, plus brand and creator work |
| Price under about $100 with little repeat value | Direct-to-offer Sales campaign only | No awareness of any kind. See `ecommerce.md` |

The main campaign always follows the consolidated structure in `campaign-setup.md` (one campaign, one ad set, many ads, warm and cold together). The omnipresent content campaign is an extra campaign with its own rules. [v054, v043, v042]

## Local business playbook

### Why local is a different game
- Competition is three or four local advertisers, not national brands, and most of them run weak ads with no real offer. Being the best advertiser in the area is realistic, so the offer and basic creative quality decide results more than account mechanics. [v077, v126]
- Word of mouth is far stronger locally. A customer brings repeat visits and referrals, so it is rational to pay more for the first customer than first-visit revenue suggests, provided the service is good. Poor service makes word of mouth compound against the business: a 7/10 loses to the 9/10 down the road, and ad costs rise. [v077, v126, v069]
- Audiences are small. Rules written for national accounts (audience size targets, ads that run unchanged for six months, interest layering) do not transfer. [v126]
- Growth is capped by capacity and population, not by return. Expect requests to pause because the diary is full; that is success, not campaign failure. Grow by opening more locations or teams and repeating the playbook. Do not switch to an online or national model, which swaps weak local rivals for specialist national ones. [v126, v120]
- A local service with high customer value is the easiest business type on Meta. Where there is a choice, aim for a lifetime value (LTV) of at least $5,000. [v120]

### Location and targeting
1. Set location under the audience "Controls". It is a hard boundary; interests, age range and (since November 2026) gender are only suggestions. Never leave location for Meta to work out. [v070, v002]
2. Pin the town and set the radius to how far customers will travel, or how far the business will travel. The pin defaults to +25 miles; adjust anywhere from city only to about 50 miles (gym: city only or a small radius; specialist project work: 25 miles or more). Check the map for the wrong entity (a district instead of the city) and for awkward geography such as a radius that crosses an estuary. [v076, v117, v195]
3. Do not widen the radius to give Meta a bigger audience. An audience of 50,000 is fine if that is the real catchment, because leads that cannot be served are worthless. [v126, v132]
4. Untick "Reach more people likely to respond to your ads". It also delivers to people who are merely interested in the selected places. Meta warns that results will be worse (the example showed 6.7% lower cost per result with it on); ignore the warning for the same reason as point 3. [v049]
5. Add no narrowing. In a town of 50,000–100,000 people any interest or demographic limit leaves too few people, so target everyone in the area. If suggestions are added in a new account, keep them very large. [v126, v070]
6. Several locations: location is the one targeting variable still worth splitting, because it is a hard control. Use separate ad sets or campaigns per city or area. [v043]

Since 2023 the only location behaviour is "living in or recently in this location", so visitors leak into local campaigns, worst in tourist and commuter cities. The symptom is leads from other cities, regions or countries. [v117, v097]

| Fix | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Geofence | Ad set > Locations: add the target area (for example city + 15 miles). Add each surrounding town with a radius that just touches the target circle (10–12 miles in the example), open the dropdown beside it and choose "Exclude location". Repeat all the way round. | Use when more than about 5% of leads or impressions come from outside. Can cut waste from 20–30% of budget to about 2–5%. It also drops a few residents who recently visited an excluded zone; accept that, since a local business has more prospects than it can serve. [v117, v097] |
| Call-out | Open the primary text or the video with the place name ("West Londoners, ..."). | Only locals respond, and in a Leads or Sales campaign those conversions steer delivery toward residents over time. Combine with geofencing; if the call-out reads as forced, rely on the geofence. [v117] |
| Form question | Add a location question to the instant form with conditional logic that ends the form for out-of-area answers. | Out-of-area people never register as leads, so Meta stops learning from them. See `lead-generation.md`. [v011] |

After any fix, check where leads actually come from. [v117]

### Offers
The offer is the main lever, because local rivals mostly run "free quote" or nothing. Over-the-top offers that audiences ignore in crowded national markets still stand out locally. Full offer design is in `offers-and-economics.md`; the local patterns are: [v077, v126, v133]

- **Cheap paid first step (tripwire).** Sell the existing entry product at a deep discount directly from the ad, with no lead magnet in front. Full price is too big a commitment for cold traffic, and free attracts people who will never buy the follow-on and wastes staff time. State it as the full price with the percentage off ("£85, 80% off"), never as the bare low price. Expect to lose money on the first session; the return comes from the follow-on plan, repeats and referrals. [v077]
- **Guarantees aimed at the industry's known fear.** "We never charge more than our quote" or "finished on time or 20% back" work for trades because competitors do overrun. [v126]
- **Deadline plus real scarcity.** Give an end date and a limited number derived from actual capacity (staff × sessions per day). Scarcity is under-used and reliably lifts response. Keep it true: a local audience sees the business repeatedly, and fake scarcity damages the brand. [v077, v041, v385]
- **Rotate the reason, keep the offer.** When the deadline passes, run the next sale with a new reason (season, business birthday, a trade's awareness week, Black Friday, New Year). Prospects pay far less attention than the owner assumes; a differently framed sale every month is workable. [v077]
- **Local events as hooks.** Tie a promotion to a local festival or race week even if unrelated to the business. Each runs two weeks to a month, which also forces fresh creative. [v126]
- **Know which segment the offer pulls.** A discounted physiotherapy assessment attracts people with nagging long-term problems, not acute injuries, who book anyway. Write the ads for the segment the offer actually moves. [v077]

### Creative
- Say the place. Name the town or area in the creative and the copy, show a local landmark in the first seconds, and include the address if there are premises. Generic ads do not catch local attention. [v126, v104]
- Make proof visibly local: reviewer's name and town under the quote. People assume a neighbour's experience will match their own. [v126]
- Reaction compilations are the strongest format found for services with a visible result: about 5 seconds per clip of customers reacting straight after the service, many customer types, quick cuts. In the physiotherapy case this more than doubled return on ad spend (ROAS) with the same offer and budget; lengths from 15 seconds to 2 minutes all worked, with about 90 seconds best. The best versions did not mention the offer in the video, so the primary text, headline and landing page must explain it. Get consent to record, then ask to use good clips, optionally with a discount as thanks. [v077, v111]
- Demonstrate the result on camera when it is visible (dirty to clean). Founder on camera plus demonstration covers two styles in one ad. [v041]
- Match production to positioning. Phone-shot, authentic footage suits trades and a local trainer; a surgeon, lawyer or premium installer needs more polish. Either way, modest effort beats the do-it-yourself creative most local rivals run. [v041, v126]
- A structure that ran for about 2.5 years (37 seconds): location call-out plus problem call-out, agitate the problem, introduce the real practitioner with credentials, reviews, offer, scarcity, specific instruction ("click the Get offer button below"). End every ad with a stated next step. [v041]
- Do not let Meta's generative backgrounds or image expansion alter photos of real work; it would show things that did not happen. [v024]
- Refresh faster than a national account. If everyone in the area sees the ad about once a week, it is tired after about a month. Rotate formats, imagery and videos; the local-event calendar supplies reasons. [v126]

### Structure, budget and pacing
- Use a Leads or Sales campaign with one offer, usually one campaign and one ad set. Do not run awareness, traffic or engagement campaigns to "get the name out"; Meta optimises literally and delivers reach or clicks, not customers. [v042]
- Set the allowable cost from customer value, not first-visit revenue. Wanting a £6 customer for a £25 haircut ignores hundreds of pounds of repeat visits. Allowable cost per lead (CPL) = customer value × share willing to spend on acquisition × lead-to-customer rate. See `offers-and-economics.md`. [v001, v120]
- Budget is front-loaded. Spend is needed to fill the diary; once repeat visits and referrals carry it, taper the budget and move it to the next location. Do not switch off completely. [v077]
- Hold spend steady when a capacity limit is in sight instead of scaling and then pausing. [v114]
- With low conversion volume, change the campaign no more than once every 7–14 days and judge on conversions accumulated, not days elapsed. See `campaign-setup.md`. [v042]
- Instant form or website, form questions and follow-up speed are covered in `lead-generation.md`.

### Expectations
- Early customers may only break even. Results improve as word of mouth builds and later ads land on people who have already heard of the business, so do not kill a local campaign because first-month customer acquisition cost (CAC) looks high. [v126]
- Long sales cycles hide results: for a kitchen fitter, leads from months one and two close in months four and five. [v385]
- Some mature local businesses reach the point of needing no ads for a location. [v126, v077]

### Case studies

| Business | What was done | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Physiotherapy chain opening two clinics in a new city | Full-price offer and free lead magnets had both failed over about 9 months. Replaced with the £85 assessment at 80% off (£17), month-end deadline, about 40 places, reaction-compilation videos | £29 cost per assessment sold; 1 in 3 bought a plan of about 12 sessions at £65 (about £780); CAC = (£29 × 3) − (£17 × 3) = £36, about 22x; both clinics fully booked within 60 days [v077] |
| Interior design firm | Leads campaign judged against customer value | Customer about £75,000; 10% allowable = £7,500 CAC; 1 in 12 leads closes = about £620 allowable CPL; actual CPL under 10% of that [v120] |

## Service, high-ticket and expertise-based businesses

### Why high customer value is the advantage
- Meta is an auction: advertisers reaching the same people pay roughly the same cost per thousand impressions (CPM) whatever they sell. A customer worth 10 times more usually costs only 3–4 times more to acquire. [v120]
- Worked example: a $10,000 service with $50 leads closing 1 in 8 costs $400 per customer, a 25x return. Reported 70x–100x results come from businesses like this. [v095]
- High value forgives weak ads (a £40,000 job stays profitable at £5,000 per customer), which is why many such accounts run mediocre creative; better creative is still the cheapest gain. [v041]
- If value is low, raise it before fixing ads: recurring service, maintenance plan, membership. That also decides whether omnipresent content is viable. [v131, v001]

### Funnel by price
The price matters less than the next step the ad asks for. Ask for the furthest step that a cold prospect will take and that can be tracked inside Meta's 7-day click window. [v091, v536]

| Price or customer value | What the ad asks for | Funnel |
|---|---|---|
| Under about $100 | The purchase | One step, ad to sales page. Extra steps cost more than the customer is worth. [v095] |
| About $100 to $1,000 | A low-commitment step: free consultation, quote, audit, or a cheap paid first session | Ad to landing page or instant form, then the sale in person or by phone. Keep it to two or three steps; small audiences cannot feed long funnels. [v385, v077] |
| $1,000 and above (lifetime, not only first sale) | The same lead step | Leads campaign, plus an omnipresent content campaign alongside. [v092, v054] |
| $5,000 and above | A call, application or proposal request; never the sale itself | Selling directly is unlikely to work. Leads objective, because the sale happens offline and outside the attribution window. Warm prospects with repeated proof, demonstration and several calls to action over time. [v095, v091] |
| Around $50,000 | A conversation | Works only with substantial warming and brand; unknown sellers get no response at this price. [v095, v054] |

Related rules:
- Position the front-end offer at one end: free or very cheap and trivial to deliver, or high-ticket with a high-touch sales process that one client pays for. The mid-range is where most competitors sit and its margin cannot fund a long sales process. [v033]
- When the ads are already decent and growth has stalled, look at the offer line-up. Adding a £3,000 done-with-you tier beside a £21,000 done-for-you service took leads from about 45 to over 300 a month and revenue from £128k to £386k a month in five months, while done-for-you leads fell only 9%. Always check a new tier for cannibalisation. [v110]
- Click-to-WhatsApp can replace or precede the sales call for higher-ticket or unfamiliar offers; in the UK it works better for high ticket than low. Webinar and lead-magnet funnels suit high-end courses and coaching. Mechanics for all of these are in `lead-generation.md`. [v033, v051, v349]
- If the real conversion is too rare for Meta to learn from, optimise for the lowest funnel event that reaches about 20–25 a week (purchase, else booked call, else webinar attendee, else registrant). See `campaign-setup.md`. [v013]

### What the ads must do
The bigger the ask, the more convincing the ad has to carry.

- **Format follows the ask.** A free call or lead magnet can be sold with an image. A high-ticket service to cold people needs video with proof, the upside and how the service works; viewers who drop out were unlikely buyers. Statics now win more often overall, but video still wins for expertise and trust-based offers to cold audiences, while warm audiences and known personal brands respond better to statics. Run both in the same ad set. [v003, v096]
- **Proof in nearly every ad**, from a quarter of the ad to almost all of it: video testimonials, results shown on screen (filmed, not screenshotted, in sceptical markets), finished-work clips, sequential before and after. Capture proof as a standing process; a stock of happy customers is the one advantage a new competitor cannot copy. [v111]
- **Show how it works.** Portfolio, process, what is included, how much input the client gives, what happens after they enquire. Not knowing how to hire a provider is itself a barrier. [v131, v385]
- **A real person.** Put the founder or practitioner in some ads; service and expertise ads are usually face to camera. [v385, v079]
- **Qualify in the copy.** Naming the job size ("40K+ install") or the customer types served repels bargain hunters and makes the right people pay attention. [v041]
- **Handle objections in the script** in sceptical markets (no time, too expensive), each answered by the offer. [v041]
- **Production value matches the price.** Cheap banners, meme edits and AI voiceover undermine premium positioning; test such styles reluctantly and keep them only if cost per result is far better. [v041]
- **Never fabricate proof.** No AI actors claiming to be customers. [v056]

Prospects check before they enquire, so the surroundings matter as much as the ad:
- Reviews: make collection someone's job. A slow decline despite steady new creative is often a reviews problem, not an account problem. [v069, v068]
- Profiles: keep the Facebook Page and Instagram profile alive with recent posts and answered comments. A near-empty profile (45 followers, no posts) costs conversions on trust-dependent offers. [v084, v095]
- Do not exclude existing customers or leads. They are a small share of spend, they repurchase and refer, and they leave positive comments on the ads. [v054, v084]

### Reading results on long sales cycles
- Meta's longest click window is 7 days, so Ads Manager under-reports sales that take longer; in one example it showed about £3,000 of revenue where £15,000 was generated. Do not judge these campaigns on in-platform ROAS alone. See `tracking-and-attribution.md`. [v075]
- Judge ads on cost per qualified lead and back-end close rate. High-ticket on a small budget may need a month or more to compare four ads. [v133]
- Follow-up speed and persistence often move revenue more than lead cost. See `lead-generation.md`. [v133]

## The omnipresent content campaign

**This is the deliberate exception to the consolidation rule.** Everywhere else the instruction is one campaign, one ad set, many diverse ads, Advantage+ placements, and follow Meta's recommendations. The omnipresent content campaign (OPC) breaks each of those on purpose, because its goal is not conversions now but repeated, varied exposure to the same people. Meta's warnings about fragmented ad sets and manual placements, and reps advising consolidation, do not apply to it. They do apply to every other campaign in the account. [v054, v043]

### What it is and who it is for
OPC shows a fairly small group of people about two different ads a day, every day, for months, so that the business is the known and trusted choice when the prospect is ready. It replaces nothing: a Leads or Sales campaign runs alongside to collect the demand. [v054]

Check fit before recommending it. Probably more than half of all businesses do not qualify. [v054]

| Fits | Does not fit |
|---|---|
| Customer worth at least $1,000 over time (lifetime, not necessarily the first sale). Every repeat impression is paid for and must be recouped. | $20–50 one-off purchases, impulse ecommerce |
| Expertise-based or trust-based: coaches, consultants, agencies, professional services, courses, bespoke or high-end products | Low-value services (car wash) |
| Timing-dependent purchases, where the buyer is only ready at some unpredictable moment, in three weeks or three years | Mass-awareness consumer brands |
| Already running, or about to run, a conversion campaign | Low priority on a small budget (under about $3,000 a month): get the conversion campaign working first [v042] |

### The recipe
Build one ad set completely, then duplicate it. Values are from the newest version of the strategy. [v054]

| Level | Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign | Objective | Awareness by default. Engagement for a very niche market or a local business with a small population | Both now offer frequency control, which the strategy cannot work without. Awareness gives more reach; Engagement fewer people and more interaction. The detailed settings below are specified for Awareness. |
| Campaign | Buying type | Auction | Reservation buys cheaper but lower-quality reach and suits only very large budgets |
| Campaign | Budget | "Advantage campaign budget" off; budgets at ad set level | Campaign budget would pour spend into one ad set and people would see one ad |
| Campaign | A/B test | Off | |
| Campaign | Special ad categories | Declare if they apply | Required even though nothing is sold directly. See `account-health-and-policy.md` |
| Ad set | Performance goal | "Maximize reach of ads" | |
| Ad set | Frequency control | "Cap", not "Target". Change the default "2 times every 7 days" to 1 time every 7 days | Target is an average, so some people see far more; Cap is strict |
| Ad set | Budget | Daily, identical in every ad set. 1 to 5 (dollars or pounds) per ad set per day is the normal start, so about 14 to 70 a day for 14 ad sets | Small budgets are deliberate: they keep delivery mostly inside the warm audiences |
| Ad set | Schedule | No end date, no budget scheduling | Value accumulates over months and years |
| Ad set | Cost per result goal, dynamic creative, value rules for audiences | Blank, off, none | Exact control over which ad is shown |
| Ad set | Location (Controls) | Everywhere the business can serve. Local: town plus realistic radius. International: core markets with similar CPMs and language only | A reach goal sends delivery to the cheapest country, which converts worst |
| Ad set | Minimum age, languages | Change only for a legal or practical reason | |
| Ad set | Exclusions | None. Keep customers and current leads in | They repurchase and refer, and cost little |
| Ad set | Audience suggestion | Every warm custom audience; age, gender and detailed targeting left open. Do not click "Further limit the reach of your ads" | Meta starts with the warm people and extends to similar cold people as budget allows, a warm and cold hybrid |
| Ad set | Placements | Remove Audience Network and Messenger. Feeds, Stories and Reels carry delivery; a little in-stream and search is fine. Untick "Allow limited spend to excluded placements" | With a reach goal Meta buys the cheapest impressions, which build no relationship [v054, v047] |
| Ad | Count | One ad per ad set | |

**Placements as of late 2026.** Meta began removing ad-set-level placement selection in September 2026, and this hits reach-optimised campaigns hardest. If "Manual placements" is gone from the account, use Advertising settings > Account controls > "Placement controls" to block Audience Network for the whole account, or a value rule set with criteria type Placement and "Decrease bid by" 90% for Audience Network, applied to every ad set. The earlier OPC instruction not to use value rules referred to audience weighting and predates this change. Value rules were first limited to conversion-optimised campaigns, so confirm the section appears on a reach-optimised ad set. Afterwards open Breakdown > Placement and confirm Audience Network is not taking the impressions; wrongly built OPC campaigns have sent most of their delivery there. [v010, v073, v054]

### Number of ad sets and the frequency rule
- Standard build: duplicate the finished ad set 13 times for 14 identical ad sets, each holding one different ad. [v054]
- 14 ads, each capped at 1 impression per 7 days, gives each person about 2 different ads a day and never the same ad twice in a week. In practice some days bring three or four and some none. [v054, v131]
- Separate ad sets are required because frequency control sits at ad set level, and inside one ad set Meta would concentrate delivery on its favourite ad. [v054]
- With a different number of ads, keep two a day: **cap window in days = number of ads ÷ 2.** More than two a day causes fatigue. [v054, v131]

| Ads (= ad sets) | Cap |
|---|---|
| 10 | 1 time every 5 days |
| 12 | 1 time every 6 days |
| 14 | 1 time every 7 days |
| 16 | 1 time every 8 days |

- Several local branches: bundle the locations into the one campaign or build a separate OPC campaign per location; start with one. Do not add per-location ad sets inside a campaign. [v054]

### Audiences and size limits
Create these first under All tools > Audiences > Create audience > Custom audience. Choose the broadest event and the longest retention every time; Meta already knows who inside each audience is warmest. [v054]

| Audience | Setting |
|---|---|
| Website | "All website visitors", 180 days (needs the pixel) |
| Customer list | Customers and email subscribers, ideally as two lists; at least 100 people; include a value column for customers; re-upload regularly unless synced |
| Video | "People who viewed at least 3 seconds", many videos selected, 365 days; add new videos over time |
| Lead form | "People who opened this form", 90 days |
| Instagram | "Everyone who engaged with this professional account", 365 days |
| Facebook Page | "People who engaged with your Page", 365 days |

- There is no minimum size. A new business with one video and 120 views can start; the audience builds from the first day of advertising. [v054]
- With about 20,000–30,000 or more people in warm audiences, most impressions stay inside them at small budgets. Below that, Meta fills with similar cold people, which is the intended hybrid. [v131, v054]
- The control on audience size is the budget, not targeting. Raising budgets pushes delivery further into cold people, so raise them as the warm audiences grow or when wider reach is wanted. [v054]
- A town of about 100,000–120,000 people can be the whole audience from the start. [v131]
- Lookalikes are cold audiences, not warm ones, and do not need to be built; Meta extrapolates from the custom audiences. [v054, v131]

### Ad mix
For 14 ads, a 4 / 4 / 4 / 2 split; 4 / 3 / 4 / 3 is also used. Adjust to the business, but calls to action are always the smallest group, and softer than in a conversion campaign. [v054, v057, v131]

| Type | Count | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Value | 4 | Information the prospect can use without buying, to get something they want or avoid something they do not. Ideally video; information-dense images can work |
| Demonstration | 4 | What the service is, what is included, the process, how long it takes, how much input is needed, what happens next, how it is made. Most important for new, bespoke or unfamiliar offers; fewer if the product is understood |
| Testimonial | 4 | Customer videos first, written reviews as images, credibility markers such as a review-site rating. One per customer segment, so each prospect sees someone like them |
| Call to action | 2 | The next step, stated directly (book a call), with real urgency or scarcity where it exists. Softer steps such as a webinar or lead magnet also fit here |

- Vary format and look across the set; variety is what makes two impressions a day tolerable. [v131]
- Only run ads the business is proud of. The campaign exists to build a relationship, and constant selling sours it. [v054]
- Launch order: switch on the value ad sets first, the others a day or so later, the call-to-action ad sets last. After the first cycle the order mixes, which is fine. [v054]
- "Ad sequencing" (ad set level; Awareness or Engagement only; needs ad set budgets, frequency control and a lifetime budget; not in every account) would allow a fixed order inside one ad set. Do not rebuild OPC around it: a fixed weekly sequence repeats or demands 14 new ads a week, and a mixed order is preferable. Use it only if order truly matters. [v054, v057]

### Refresh
- Replace all the ads roughly every 3 to 6 months, using what drew engagement and positive comments in the last batch. [v054]
- Adjust for audience turnover. Audiences that renew themselves (wedding or newborn markets) need fewer refreshes; static audiences, including a small local population, need more. Comments along the lines of "seeing this all the time" mean it is due. [v054, v126]

### How to judge it
There is no hard metric early, and cost per result means nothing here. Look for: [v054]
- the tone of comments on the ads;
- prospects and customers saying they see the business everywhere;
- improving results in the conversion campaign that runs alongside;
- follower and organic engagement growth;
- over time, easier sales calls and less price resistance.

### How long before it pays back
- Treat the first six months as an act of faith rather than data. [v057]
- The first visible effect is a lift in the direct-offer campaigns, because the same people see both. Lead flow then grows at 6, 12 and 18 months, and after about two years some businesses need little other advertising. [v131]
- It keeps improving the longer it runs, so it is never judged as worked or failed at a point in time. [v054]

### Audit checks
When an account shows an Awareness or Engagement campaign with 10–16 single-ad ad sets, equal small daily budgets and frequency caps, treat it as OPC. Do not recommend consolidating it. Check instead:

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Business does not meet the fit criteria | Recommend ending it and moving the budget to the conversion campaign |
| No conversion campaign running alongside | Add one; OPC alone produces no short-term revenue |
| Campaign budget on, or unequal ad set budgets | Switch to equal ad set budgets |
| "Target" selected, or cap left at 2 per 7 days, or window not equal to ads ÷ 2 | Correct the cap |
| More than one ad in an ad set, or dynamic creative on | One fixed ad per ad set |
| Placement breakdown dominated by Audience Network | Apply the placement fix above |
| Customers excluded, or hard-limited audiences | Remove exclusions; use warm audiences as suggestions |
| Same ads for more than 6 months, or fatigue comments | Full refresh |
| End date or lifetime budget set | Daily budgets, no end date |

## Brand and personal brand
- Brand is the largest multiplier outside the ad account. Clients fronted by a major celebrity reached 10:1 to 12:1 where the benchmark for similar campaigns was about 4:1, and a known name lets ordinary ads beat a stranger's better ones. A $50,000 offer from an unknown gets no response; from a known figure it sells out. [v133, v069, v054]
- It matters more as AI-made creative floods feeds: people fall back on names and faces they trust. [v053, v019]
- Two routes: build (own content over years, OPC for businesses that fit) or borrow (creators and partnership ads, which work in weeks). Mechanics of creator work are in `creative.md`. [v013, v133]
- Personal-brand ads depend on recognition. Use the person's face consistently, prefer statics for audiences that already know them, and reject AI image variations that generate a different-looking person. A more familiar, even joking tone is appropriate to followers. [v003, v097, v095]
- Hiring an actor instead of the owner is fine and removes a bottleneck, except where the person is the brand. [v104]
- Protect the positioning in direct-response ads too. Clickbait tone, meme edits and visibly AI-made content can win short term and cost a premium brand over time; prefer a style that performs nearly as well without that cost. [v046, v041]
- Followers are a credibility signal, not a sales channel. Do not run follower campaigns in order to sell organically. Do make sure the profile does not look empty: for a new account run a small, short follower campaign (Instagram: Traffic objective with profile visits; Facebook: Engagement objective with the Page as conversion location). [v095, v087]
- Meta One (the paid subscription that extends Meta Verified, as of October 2026): the Advanced tier at $50 a month per platform is the recommended level for businesses that depend on Meta. The verified badge supports trust for expertise-based offers, and the tier includes one human support case a month. See `account-health-and-policy.md`. [v009]
- A small advertiser builds brand by going narrow: being the obvious choice for one segment is worth more than being vaguely known by many. [v133, v042]

## Organic content and paid ads
- **Organic is free creative testing.** Post Reels and images (mainly Instagram). Take posts with clearly above-average engagement, add a 5–10 second call to action at the end of the video, or a text overlay and headline on a static, and run them as ads. Organic posts usually lack a call to action because it hurts organic reach, and ads need one. It does not always carry over, but the concept or hook can be reused. The content has to relate closely to what is sold, and it works better with an existing following. [v042, v029]
- Build these as new ads in Ads Manager with "Create ad", not "Use existing post". Never boost from the Facebook or Instagram app: fewer options, and about 30% extra cost on iOS. [v049, v025]
- **Organic feeds the warm audiences.** Video viewers and profile engagers are retained for 365 days, are retargeted by default in conversion campaigns, and are the core of the OPC audience. [v054, v070]
- **Paid feeds organic.** OPC raises follower counts and organic engagement, and combining the two amplifies both, though OPC works without organic content. [v054]
- **Launches use all three channels together:** teaser posts, emails to the list before and during, simple lightly edited retargeting ads to warm people, and launch-only bonuses for urgency. Alternate calls to action between offers across organic posts; do not stack several in one. [v110]
- **Manage ad comments** in Meta Business Suite every couple of days: delete negative ones, answer questions, reply to positive ones so they rise. Comments are public proof on the ad. [v084]
- Organic alone is not a growth plan on Meta, since reach is limited (more so on Facebook than Instagram). Advertise the offer directly and gain followers as a by-product. [v095]

## Outdated advice to ignore

| Old rule | Current rule | Why it changed |
|---|---|---|
| OPC must use the Awareness (formerly Reach) objective | Awareness by default; Engagement allowed for niche or small local markets | Frequency control was added to Engagement [v054] |
| OPC targets a hard-limited warm audience, or a tight cold audience of 50,000–100,000 (later under 250,000) built with interests and "Narrow audience" | All warm custom audiences as suggestions, everything else open; the budget limits spread | Hard limits now give worse results, and interests and gender can no longer be made hard limits at all [v054, v010, v002] |
| Consolidate the 14 ad sets into one using ad sequencing and a lifetime budget | Keep separate ad sets with daily budgets | Sequencing is not in every account, repeats weekly and is predictable [v054] |
| Frequency "Target" is probably better than "Cap" | Cap for OPC | Target is an average and lets some people see far more [v054] |
| Choose manual placements at ad set level for OPC | Still the aim, but as of late 2026 the control is being removed; use account-level placement controls or a placement value rule | Platform change [v010] |
| Choose "People living in this location" | Option removed in 2023; geofence, call out the place, and ask location in the form | Platform change [v117] |
| Local campaign with five ad sets (10% lookalike, two interests, open, retargeting) | One ad set, everyone in the area, warm and cold together; split only by location | Targeting inputs are suggestions, so audience tests are not real tests [v043] |
| Never ask for anything directly if the service costs over about $100 | Go direct to a low-commitment step (consultation, quote, cheap paid session); add warming above about $1,000 and expect direct selling to fail above about $5,000 | Most working campaigns are one step to an offer; what matters is the size of the ask [v095] |
| Warm people with traffic, reach or video-view campaigns before any conversion campaign; put 10–20% of budget into brand awareness | Conversion campaign from day one. Awareness only as OPC for businesses that fit, run alongside | Meta optimises literally; awareness without the OPC structure returns nothing measurable for small businesses [v042, v138] |
| Avoid testimonials in ads except for local businesses | Proof in nearly every ad for any service business | Proof-led creative more than doubled results in testing [v111] |
| Build followers first, then sell to them organically | Sell directly; keep a minimum credible following | Organic reach is too limited to pay back [v095] |
